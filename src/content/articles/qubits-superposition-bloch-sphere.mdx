Whereas classical computer bits exist in a deterministic state of either $0$ or $1$, quantum computing leverages the laws of quantum mechanics. A **Qubit (Quantum Bit)** can exist in a linear combination of both states simultaneously—a phenomenon known as **Quantum Superposition**.

---

## 1. Summary & Key Takeaways

- **Quantum Superposition**: A single qubit state $|\psi\rangle$ is represented mathematically as:
  $$|\psi\rangle = \alpha |0\rangle + \beta |1\rangle$$
  where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are complex probability amplitudes satisfying $|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1$.
- **Hadamard Gate ($H$)**: Transforms ground state $|0\rangle$ into equal superposition $\frac{|0\rangle + |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$, granting a $50\%$ chance of measuring either $0$ or $1$.
- **Measurement Collapse**: Observing a qubit forces its wave function to collapse probabilistically into a single classical state ($|0\rangle$ or $|1\rangle$).
- **The Bloch Sphere**: A geometric representation of single-qubit quantum states as points on the surface of a unit sphere.

## 3. Mathematical Foundations & Quantum Logic Gates

Single-qubit quantum gates are represented as $2 \times 2$ unitary matrices $U$ acting on state vectors:

### Hadamard Gate ($H$)
Places ground states into equal superposition:
$$H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad H|0\rangle = \frac{|0\rangle + |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$$

### Pauli-X Gate (Bit Flip)
Acts as the quantum equivalent of a classical NOT gate:
$$X = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad X|0\rangle = |1\rangle, \quad X|1\rangle = |0\rangle$$

### Pauli-Z Gate (Phase Flip)
Flips the quantum phase angle $\phi$ without altering measurement probabilities:
$$Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad Z\left(\frac{|0\rangle + |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{|0\rangle - |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$$

## 4. Quantum Computing vs Classical Computing Matrix

| Property | Classical Computing | Quantum Computing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Information Unit** | Bit ($0$ or $1$) | Qubit ($|\psi\rangle = \alpha |0\rangle + \beta |1\rangle$) |
| **State Space ($N$ units)** | $1$ state out of $2^N$ | All $2^N$ states in superposition simultaneously |
| **Logic Operations** | Boolean Gates (AND, OR, NOT) | Unitary Quantum Matrix Gates ($H$, $X$, $CNOT$) |
| **Search Complexity** | $O(N)$ Unsorted Search | $O(\sqrt{N})$ Grover's Quantum Search |
